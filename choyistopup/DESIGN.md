# Choyis TopUp Design System

## Direction

An international utility-payment counter translated to the web: immediate service recognition, strong operational hierarchy, and calm financial confidence. It uses Choyis navy, electric blue and clear cyan on a bright neutral field. The standard is the transaction clarity of Paytm and BuyPower, with larger typography and less clutter.

## Composition

- The first viewport contains the brand, concise product promise, service selector and the real active checkout.
- The service selector is a responsive icon rail: four columns on small screens and seven columns on wide screens.
- Only one transaction panel is visible at a time.
- Marketing content never competes with the active task.

## Colour

- Canvas: `#f4f8ff`
- Surface: `#ffffff`
- Ink: `#071a3d`
- Secondary ink: `#49617f`
- Primary: `#075ee6`
- Primary deep: `#0648ad`
- Cyan: `#08b9e6`
- Positive: `#087a55`
- Warning: `#b76500`
- Borders: `#dce6f3`

Service colours identify categories but never replace labels.

## Typography

Use the existing workhorse sans-serif stack. Body copy is at least 16px. Important descriptions are 18px. Inputs and buttons use 16px or larger. Headings use compact negative tracking no tighter than `-0.03em`.

## Components

- Service controls use bespoke two-tone line icons in a shared 24px grid.
- Transaction inputs are at least 52px tall.
- Cards use either a border or a shadow as their primary separation, not both equally.
- Status messages pair icons, headings and recovery instructions.
- Provider names and live catalogue content remain data, never decorative mock content.

## Responsive behaviour

- Mobile leads with the service selector and active transaction without a large marketing detour.
- The service rail wraps into a compact grid rather than a clipped horizontal navigation.
- Desktop places product context beside the transaction workspace, while the form remains the visual anchor.
- Long service names wrap without reducing type below the accessibility floor.

## Motion

One restrained workspace transition uses opacity and vertical movement. All content remains visible by default, and reduced-motion preferences disable the transition.
